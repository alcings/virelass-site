Gate hardware

Unmanned lanes, the same rules

A Virelass gate controller runs the lanes that have no guard. It checks codes, QR passes, tags and plates with exactly the rules the guard's phone uses — dates and hours, entries, the watchlist, emergencies.

Gate controller

A Raspberry Pi or small computer at the gate, wired to the barrier. It keeps a copy of the estate's passes and rules and decides on its own.

Keypads and QR scanners

USB readers that type like a keyboard, or weatherproof Wiegand keypads. Visitors type their code or show their QR.

Resident tags

Windscreen stickers or RFID cards on a Wiegand reader. The estate approves each car and its tag; it opens for its owner.

Number-plate cameras

ANPR cameras on the estate network. Approved resident cars open by plate; visitors booked with their plate are checked like a code.

Offline by design

The gate does not wait for the internet

  • Every decision is made on the box, from data it refreshes every few minutes and whenever something changes
  • Residents' eight-digit offline codes work with no connection at all
  • Controllers at different gates share what they let in over the estate's own network, so a single-use code works once
  • Everything is uploaded to the gate log as soon as the connection is back

Safe by design

Hard to fool, easy to recover

  • Codes are never stored in the clear; QR passes are signed by the estate
  • Remote openings run only when signed by the estate, and are logged with a reason
  • Keypads lock after repeated wrong codes; tamper switches are reported
  • A lost box is revoked and its secrets rotated from the web app
  • Lockdowns and evacuations reach the controller straight away; evacuations hold the exits open
